📖 Introduction & User Guide

Read this first — how to use Adsorblab and understand your results
Welcome to Adsorblab | ArslanENV

Adsorblab | ArslanENV is a complete, browser-based research dashboard designed to help students, teachers, and researchers perform full adsorption studies — from raw data entry to publication-ready results — without needing Excel, Origin, or any paid software. It works entirely in your browser, stores data locally, and requires no internet connection after the first load.

No backend Works offline Free to use Educational
Who is this for?
  • BS / MSc / MPhil / PhD Students working on adsorption experiments
  • Teachers & Professors teaching environmental chemistry or water treatment
  • Researchers in Environmental Science, Chemistry, Materials Science
  • Laboratory Technicians handling UV-Vis and adsorption studies
How to Use — Step-by-Step Workflow
Step 1: Open the dashboard — demo data loads automatically so you can explore and learn.
Step 2: Create your own study → Dashboard → New Study button
Step 3: Fill Research Setup with experiment details (adsorbent, adsorbate, objective).
Step 4: Enter Spectrophotometer standards → click Calculate Standard Curve to get regression equation.
Step 5: Enter experimental data in Equilibrium, Isotherm, Kinetics, Thermodynamics.
Step 6: Click Calculate in each module → graphs and results appear automatically.
Step 7: Go to Model Comparison → check R², RMSE, SSE to select the best statistical fit.
Step 8: Get paper-ready summary from Research Summary module — copy & paste into your paper.
How to Read the Results

Every symbol and statistical term explained for beginners:

SymbolMeaningHow to Interpret
R²Coefficient of determination (goodness of fit)1 = perfect. 0.99+ excellent, 0.95–0.99 good, <0.90 poor
RMSERoot Mean Square ErrorLower = better fit
SSESum of Squared ErrorsLower = better fit
MAEMean Absolute ErrorLower = better fit
qe / qtAdsorption capacity (mg/g)Higher = more adsorption
qmaxMaximum capacity (Langmuir)Monolayer saturation limit
KLLangmuir constant (L/mg)Higher = stronger binding affinity
RLLangmuir separation factor0 < RL < 1 = favorable
KFFreundlich constantHigher = higher capacity
nFreundlich exponentn>1 favorable, n=1 linear, n<1 unfavorable
k1, k2Kinetic rate constantsHigher = faster adsorption
kidIntraparticle diffusion rateHigher = faster pore diffusion
ΔG°Gibbs free energy (kJ/mol)Negative = spontaneous; Positive = non-spontaneous
ΔH°Enthalpy change (kJ/mol)Positive = endothermic; Negative = exothermic
ΔS°Entropy change (J/mol·K)Positive = randomness increases
KdDistribution coefficientHigher = more adsorbate on solid

Important Notes

  1. Always check units — mg/L, mg/g, mL, g, kJ/mol, K, min⁻¹
  2. Missing values should be left blank — NOT written as 0
  3. Equilibrium time should only be declared when a plateau is clearly visible
  4. Model selection should not rely on R² alone — consider RMSE, SSE, and parameter plausibility
  5. Scientific interpretation is your responsibility — the tool only does the math
  6. For temperature, always use Kelvin (K) where required — the tool auto-converts °C → K

General Adsorption Guide

What is adsorption and which study is used for what?

What is adsorption?

Adsorption is the adhesion of atoms, ions, or molecules from a gas, liquid, or dissolved solid to a surface. It differs from absorption, where a substance diffuses into a liquid or solid.

  • Adsorbent: The solid material that provides the surface for adsorption (e.g., biochar, activated carbon).
  • Adsorbate: The substance that adheres to the adsorbent surface (e.g., dye, metal ion).
  • Desorption: The reverse process — release of adsorbate from the adsorbent.
  • Surface area & active sites: Higher surface area and more active sites generally enhance adsorption.
  • Equilibrium: The state where adsorption and desorption rates are equal.
  • Removal efficiency: Percentage of adsorbate removed from solution.
  • Adsorption capacity (qe, qt): Mass of adsorbate adsorbed per mass of adsorbent.

Experiment Guide for Beginners

Step-by-step laboratory workflow

Complete Laboratory Protocol

  1. Prepare adsorbent — washing, drying, sieving, activation
  2. Prepare adsorbate stock solution — accurate concentration
  3. Prepare standard solutions — for calibration curve
  4. Run UV-Vis calibration — determine wavelength λmax
  5. Measure samples — record absorbance values
  6. Perform equilibrium study — vary contact time
  7. Perform isotherm experiment — vary initial concentration
  8. Perform kinetic experiment — record concentration at intervals
  9. Perform thermodynamic experiment — at different temperatures
  10. Enter data into this dashboard
  11. Check regression and residuals
  12. Interpret results using the guide
  13. Prepare paper tables and figures
